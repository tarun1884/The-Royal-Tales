    The 12-Month Wedding Planning Timeline

    📅 November 2026 👤 Royal Tales Editorial Team ⏱ 9 min read

    Twelve months sounds like a long time until you start booking. The venues and vendors worth having are usually committed well before that. Here is the order things actually need to happen in.

    12 to 10 months out

    This window decides most of what follows. Date, venue and budget lock together, and the rest of the plan is shaped by them.

    • Agree the total budget and who is contributing
    • Shortlist and confirm the date, allowing for auspicious dates and family availability
    • Book the venue or venues, including room blocks
    • Draft the guest list; an approximate number is enough at this stage
    • Appoint a planner if you intend to use one

    9 to 7 months out

    The vendors who get booked first are the ones who can only do one wedding a day. Photography, catering and decor fall in this group.

    • Book photography and film
    • Confirm caterer and run a tasting
    • Appoint the decor and production team
    • Begin attire for both families; stitching timelines are longer than people expect
    • Open conversations with entertainment and choreography
    Vendors are not booked when you have chosen them. They are booked when the advance has cleared.
    — Royal Tales Team

    6 to 4 months out

    With the structure in place, this is the detail phase, and the point at which the guest list has to become a real number rather than an estimate.

    • Finalise the guest list and collect addresses
    • Design and order invitations; account for printing and dispatch time
    • Confirm hospitality: room allocation, transport, welcome kits
    • Lock the function-by-function schedule
    • Book mehendi, makeup and hair for all functions
    • Arrange permissions, licences and any noise or timing clearances

    3 to 1 months out

    Everything that has been agreed now needs to be confirmed in writing and sequenced against a clock.

    • Send invitations and begin tracking RSVPs
    • Share the final guest count with the caterer
    • Build the minute-by-minute run sheet for each function
    • Confirm every vendor's arrival time, access and setup window
    • Walk the venue with the decor and production teams
    • Settle payment schedules and confirm what is due on the day

    The final two weeks

    By now the planning should be finished. This fortnight is for confirmation, not decisions.

    • Reconfirm every vendor in writing
    • Circulate the run sheet to families and vendors
    • Brief whoever is handling on-the-day coordination
    • Prepare payment envelopes and tips in advance
    • Pack separately for each function
    • Build in a weather contingency for anything outdoors
    The last two weeks should be boring. If they are not, something was left too late.
    — Royal Tales Team
